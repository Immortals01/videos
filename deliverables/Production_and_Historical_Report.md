# ABANDONED, BUT NOT FORGOTTEN — Production & Historical Accuracy Report

## 1. Transparent Capability & Production Architecture Disclosure

Per your execution rules (*"If a particular capability is unavailable, clearly identify it and continue with everything else that can actually be completed"* and *"Do not create a slideshow and call it a finished animated movie"*):

1. **No External Text-to-Video Model Available:** This environment is a 4-core CPU Linux box without an NVIDIA GPU (`nvidia-smi` absent) and without paid text-to-video APIs (Runway, Veo, Kling, Sora). Zero paid credits or subscriptions were used.
2. **Approved Hybrid 2.5D + Procedural 3D Animation Pipeline:** As agreed at the start of production, we built a custom Python/OpenCV/NumPy/FFmpeg cinema pipeline (`production/src/`) that renders `2,160` unique `1920 × 1080` frames at `24 fps` (`90.000 s`) with continuous motion in every single shot:
   - **Reference-Locked 3D-Style Plates:** Anchor character plates of Surveyor 3 (`p_hero.jpg`, `p_camera.jpg`, `p_lowangle.jpg`) were generated directly from official NASA reference imagery, enforcing Surveyor 3's authentic **three-legged tripod landing gear**, planar antenna + solar panel mast, cylindrical survey TV camera mirror hood, and scissor-lattice surface-sampler arm (zero human eyes, face, or wheels). Subsequent shots were generated with **FLUX.1 Kontext** (`src/plates_batch.py`) locked to those hero plates and real NASA photographs so character proportions stay consistent from shot to shot.
   - **Monocular Depth Estimation + 2.5D Multi-Plane Camera Rig (`src/depth.py`, `src/fx.py`):** Every plate has a dense `Depth-Anything-V2` depth map (`plates/depth/*.npy`). Virtual cameras execute iterative perspective-correct depth parallax (`px`, `py`), depth-dependent dolly magnification (`dolly`), rack/shallow depth-of-field blur (`focus`, `dof`), and handheld engine/touchdown shake.
   - **Local Mechanical Articulation & Morphing (`src/scenes.py`):** Beyond camera parallax, Surveyor 3's sampling scoop actuates via local displacement warps (`s2_hero`, `s4_scoop`), Opportunity's robotic arm reaches toward its target outcrop (`s6_arm`), and Surveyor 3's television camera hood physically rotates from side-profile (`p_camera.jpg`) to front-facing (`k_camera_front.jpg`) in Scene 2 (`s2_camera`).
   - **True 3D Procedural Spheres, Starfields & Particle Systems:** The 4,200-star 3D perspective starfield (`fx.Starfield`), ray-cast Lambertian-lit Moon and Mars spheres textured with NASA LRO imagery (`fx.sphere`), multi-plane depth-parallaxed lunar/Martian dust motes (`fx.Dust`), live voice-driven oscilloscope trace (`waveform`), expanding radio rings, and the 45-tile `6,315 IMAGES` telemetry mosaic are rendered procedurally at 24 fps.
   - **Expressive Character Voice (`src/tts.py`):** Synthesized locally via open-source **Kokoro-82M** (`af_heart`, Apache-2.0) with per-line pacing variation—slower and more intimate on nostalgic/emotional beats, brisker on mission facts, and radio-filtered with distant lunar echo in Scene 1.
   - **100% Original Procedural Score & Sound Design (`src/audio.py`):** Synthesized from scratch in `48 kHz` stereo (inharmonic additive piano with hammer noise, 6-voice detuned string sections, spiccato ostinatos, French horns, celesta, timpani, rocket ignition/plume rumble, vernier thrusters, dual touchdown thuds, mechanical servos, Martian wind, sidechain voice ducking, convolution reverb, and two-pass EBU R128 `-16 LUFS` mastering).

## 3. Scientific & Historical Fact-Check Log (Official NASA Sources)

Verified against **NASA NSSDCA (`1967-035A`)** and **NASA Science (`science.nasa.gov/mission/surveyor-3/` & `mer-opportunity/`)**:

1. **Surveyor 3 Launch:** Launched **April 17, 1967** at `07:05:01 UTC` (`02:05:01 EST`) aboard an **Atlas-Centaur (AC-12)** from Launch Complex 36B, Cape Kennedy, Florida. **Uncrewed** robotic lunar lander (`1,026 kg` launch mass; `296 kg` landing mass).
2. **Surveyor 3 Touchdown & Double Bounce:** Landed **April 20, 1967** (`00:04:17 UTC` initial contact; `00:04:53 UTC` final rest; April 19 EST) in southeastern **Oceanus Procellarum** (`3.0162° S, 23.418° W`, inside a ~`200 m` crater). Because highly reflective lunar rocks confused the descent radar, the vernier engines stayed lit through two bounces (~`10 m` and ~`3 m` high) before Earth-commanded cutoff—reflected in Scene 3 by our **dual touchdown impact/thud** (`s3_touchdown` + `audio.py`).
3. **Instruments & Image Count:** Carried a survey television camera under an azimuth/elevation mirror hood (with extended glare visor) and a `1.5 m` scissor-lattice pantograph **surface sampler** (`12 cm × 5 cm` scoop) that operated for `18 h 22 m`, digging four trenches up to `18 cm` deep and proving lunar regolith bearing strength (~`0.7 kg/cm²`) could support an Apollo Lunar Module. *(Note: NASA educational summaries commonly cite **6,315** lunar surface pictures while the NSSDCA catalog totals **6,326** frames including calibration/engineering transmissions; the film displays `6,315 IMAGES` per the project prompt.)*
4. **Apollo 12 Encounter (Accuracy Adjustment Applied):** Apollo 12 astronauts **Charles "Pete" Conrad Jr.** and **Alan L. Bean** landed LM *Intrepid* ~`180 m` from Surveyor 3 on November 19, 1969 UTC and walked to Surveyor 3 during EVA-2 on **November 20, 1969 UTC**—**31 months (~2.5 years)** after April 1967. Accordingly, we refined the draft line *"Two years later... someone came back"* to ***"Two and a half years later... someone came back"*** (paired with the on-screen label `NOVEMBER 1969 — APOLLO 12 · 31 MONTHS LATER`). They retrieved ~`10 kg` of hardware including the TV camera (now at the Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum) and soil scoop.
5. **No Fictional Astronaut Dialogue & No Unverified Contamination Claims:** No dialogue is attributed to Conrad or Bean, and the disputed 1970 *Streptococcus mitis* survival claim (later attributed by NASA/curatorial reviews likely to post-return cleanroom contamination) is omitted completely.
6. **Opportunity (MER-B) Mission Facts:** Launched July 7, 2003; landed **January 25, 2004 UTC** (Jan 24 PST) in Eagle Crater, **Meridiani Planum** (`1.95° S, 354.47° E` / `5.53° W`). Designed for `90 sols` and `1,000 m`, Opportunity operated for **14 years, 4 months** ("nearly fifteen years") until its final communication on **June 10, 2018** during a planet-encircling dust storm in **Perseverance Valley** on the rim of Endeavour Crater, with End of Mission declared **February 13, 2019** after `45.16 km` (`28.06 miles`) driven and landmark discoveries of hematite concretions ("blueberries") and ancient liquid-water environments.
